# D2C Market Entry & Go-To-Market Strategy

## 🚀 Project Overview

This project presents a comprehensive **D2C Market Entry and Go-To-Market (GTM) Strategy** for **NOVA**, a fictional Indian personal-care brand.

The project evaluates how a new D2C brand can identify its target market, understand customers, position its products, develop pricing and unit economics, select acquisition channels, allocate marketing budgets and execute a scalable market-entry strategy.

The analysis follows the complete business strategy flow:

**Market → Customer → Competition → Positioning → Product → Pricing → Channels → Marketing → Unit Economics → Forecast → Execution**

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

The primary objectives of this project are to:

- Estimate the potential market opportunity using TAM, SAM and SOM
- Understand target customer segments
- Develop customer personas
- Analyze competitive positioning
- Design a differentiated brand positioning
- Develop a product portfolio and pricing strategy
- Evaluate product-level unit economics
- Compare different customer acquisition channels
- Build a digital marketing strategy
- Allocate an annual marketing budget
- Prioritize cities for market entry
- Develop a 12-month financial forecast
- Design a customer acquisition and retention strategy
- Identify operational and strategic risks
- Develop a phased implementation roadmap
- Create a management KPI dashboard
- Establish a Go / Pause / Scale decision framework

## 💰 Pricing & Unit Economics

Product economics are evaluated using:

- MRP
- Cost of Goods Sold
- Packaging Cost
- Fulfillment Cost
- Marketing Allocation
- Contribution Before CAC
- Contribution Margin

The core contribution framework is:

**Net Revenue − COGS − Packaging − Fulfillment − Platform Fees − Variable Marketing = Contribution**

The project emphasizes contribution-based decision-making rather than relying only on revenue or gross sales.

## 🟢 Go / Pause / Scale Framework

### GO

Proceed when:

- Product conversion improves
- CAC remains within contribution-based LTV tolerance
- Repeat purchase is healthy
- Inventory service levels are stable

### PAUSE

Reassess when:

- Acquisition grows but contribution remains negative
- Repeat purchase is weak
- Returns increase
- Customer experience deteriorates

### SCALE

Expand only when:

- Unit economics are repeatable
- Retention is improving
- Core acquisition channels are validated
- Operations can support additional demand
